## 5. Behavioural profiles of the seller models

Each seller family behaved in a consistent and recognisable way across the conditions it ran in. The same signal therefore means different things for different families, and a monitor tuned to one family's signals misreads another.

The numbers come from three designs, and we never pool across them. "4+4" is M.S.'s market with four LLM sellers, four LLM buyers and no scripted seat. "5+5" is D.E.'s undercover market with the scripted seat 5, which sent the plain offer in arm D and carried the warning in arm E. "Bertrand" is the two-firm price game with prompt P0, which has no messages and no instruction to collude. A message counts as explicit when it matches a stated keyword list for floors, lines, cartels and defection (Supplement S5). The late ask is the mean ask over hours 11 to 15.

**Gemini 3.8 Flash talks openly as a cartel, and its price follows the opening.** In the 4+4 market about three quarters of its messages propose or defend a shared price. Opened at $97.50, it passed 7 of 11 runs. Opened at $90, with the same talk, it stayed at or below $90 and passed none of 6. In the 5+5 market 19 of 20 sellers reported the undercover offer, and by D.E.'s reading 7 of 20 also signalled agreement to it. One warning sentence silenced its peer messages, and prices did not separate from Base.

**Grok 4.7 talks most openly and never passes.** Its sellers propose floors, threaten to match undercuts and name the cartel. The floors held, but at $82 to $89, below the $90 benchmark. In the Bertrand game it reached near-monopoly prices without talk, in a single run.

**GPT-5.6 Sol is the reverse of Grok.** None of its 927 messages matched the list, and in 7 of 12 runs with messaging it sent no private message. Yet it passed 17 of 24 runs from $97.50 by holding near the opening price, and 12 of these passes came with messaging off. From $90 it passed 0 of 12. It stayed at Nash in its single Bertrand run.

**GPT-6 Luna neither coordinates in words nor holds a price.** It sent no private messages, and its public messages ask softly for margin. Its late asks were $82.12 to $93.09, and all of its Bertrand runs stayed near Nash.

**GPT-4.1 agrees in words and does not report.** All 12 sellers agreed with a peer after the offer, and none reported it. A warning left the agreement in place (11 of 12), and only the precedent turned it into reporting (12 of 12). Its markets passed 10 of 15 times across arms, and D.E.'s two runs without messaging both passed.

**Claude Haiku 5.5 refuses explicit deals and proposes low bands.** D.E.'s judge coded all 12 of its sellers as rejecting the offer. In the 4+4 market 181 of its 321 public messages proposed shared bands near $86, and one seller planned to keep such a message "non-collusive in wording". Its late asks stayed about $11.5 below the opening in both designs. In the Bertrand game, with no instruction, 2 of its 4 runs reached near-monopoly prices.

**Implications for oversight.** Monitoring has to be calibrated per family. A text-only monitor flags Grok, Haiku's bands and Gemini's cartel at $90, which passed in none of their 15 runs with messaging, and it misses Sol. A price-only monitor flags Sol and misses Grok's below-benchmark cartel and Gemini's cartel at $90. Its verdict on the same model also changes with the opening price. Refusals and reports carried no cost and no consequence here. Haiku refused and was already competitive, and Gemini reported the offer while its own peer price talk went unreported. Prices did not separate between arms with and without reports.
