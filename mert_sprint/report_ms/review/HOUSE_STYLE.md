## Keep what the argument uses

R2. Cite earlier work where the argument uses it: to compare with it, to build on its model or to correct its interpretation. Cite the source inside the sentence that uses its result ("In the one-dimensional model of [cite], ..."), not in a separate sentence that describes its model. When you compare values, name the specific study and its conditions instead of remarking that published values vary with conditions or between studies. Leave out studies listed only for completeness, studies that share only the numerical method, the group's earlier work on other geometries and literature values that the analysis does not compare against. Limit: the test is use, not topic, so a critique of a cited interpretation that the argument rests on stays, such as his sentence that the mode may have been misidentified as a harmonic; caveats added on the cited study's evidence still go, under R8. (cylinder/C112, cylinder/C114, cylinder/C126, cylinder/C160, cylinder/C146, cylinder/C159, cylinder/C104)

R3. End a result on the result. Cut what is appended when the point does not need it: a general statement about the flow class, a consequence for measurements elsewhere, an explanation of the flow geometry that the result does not depend on, secondary details read off a mode such as a phase drift or a phase speed, and a detailed comparison inside the introduction's summary that needs its own definition and a concession. Inside the result, quote only the part of a range that the contrast uses ("at least 6.5 %" against 4 %, without the streamwise-velocity value). Limit: an extension that the evidence supports and the argument uses is part of the result and stays, such as the regime extension and the multi-frequency standing-wave reading that he asked for. A link to a related result also stays, as R11 asks. (cylinder/C54, cylinder/C115, cylinder/C102, cylinder/C101, cylinder/C44, cylinder/C174)

R4. Leave analysis and solver internals out of the prose when the reader does not need them to read the result: how the solver computes a stored quantity and what the method therefore cannot claim, what a finite-ensemble fluctuation amplitude means, a sampling-noise caveat after a null result whose cause is already given, and run history such as a restart. Limit: in the text, name the grid or the sampling where it describes the data ("mean profiles sampled on the finer collision grid") or names the cause of a limitation the reader needs ("too thin to probe with our sampling grid"); a caption leaves the grid out, under R19. ## Once, in its place

R5. State each result and each definition once. Cut a later sentence that restates a result the paper already gives, a sentence that restates what a defined term already says ("It is a reference value, not the resonance itself") and a generic lead-in whose content the next sentence already gives. Merge two adjacent sentences that give the same result into one. Orientation text follows the same rule: a sentence that points to a later section belongs in the outline paragraph, not in a literature paragraph; the conclusion does not close on a recap of the results taken together; and the introduction's summary does not close on how the paper extends earlier work or on an interpretation of all the cases together that it already gives. Limit: the abstract, the introduction's summary and the conclusion each state the main results for their own purpose, and his emergence-to-dominance wording stands in more than one of them. A summary across a sequence of cases stays where it closes their presentation, in the results or in the introduction's summary. The outline paragraph stays, and so does a contribution stated nowhere else. (cylinder/C143, cylinder/C158, cylinder/C121, cylinder/C90, cylinder/C122, cylinder/C56, cylinder/C48)

R6. Put method detail in the method section: how two fields are combined, the equation and the reason for a truncation rank. A results section gives only the setting it uses, such as the number of modes, one sentence on what the method is used for ("DMD is used to capture the spatial structure of the unsteadiness") and what the method shows. A passage he asks to move "as is" is moved unchanged. Only its references and the joins at both ends are repaired, such as an equation reference inside the moved text and the results sentence that pointed back to it ("the resulting modes"). Limit: which results use a method option is stated with that option in the method section under R12 and is not repeated in the results. (cylinder/C118, cylinder/C119, cylinder/C81, cylinder/C80, cylinder/C136, cylinder/C203, cylinder/C138)

## Claims at the strength of the evidence

R7. State a result by what it shows, at the strength the evidence gives. Examples: a reference value "marks the upper end of the low-frequency shock fluctuations" instead of "does not bound the spectrum"; a result that a second method recovers is present as predicted, not "the only estimate"; a finding is stated as found ("are found not to originate from wake instabilities"); a weak mode is described by what it reflects and by being much weaker than the dominant one; a secondary result closes on what it shows and what it means for the method. When all cases agree within the scatter, state the agreement for all of them, without singling out one case's small offset and without attaching a one-case detail to a sentence whose point is that all cases share a result. Limit: positive does not mean stronger, so an interpretation stays "consistent with" or "may," also when it is extended to several frequencies. (cylinder/C19, cylinder/C99, cylinder/C71, cylinder/C28, cylinder/C175, cylinder/C173, cylinder/C50, cylinder/C198)

R8. After a result or a comparison, cut a sentence or clause that only says what the result is not, or what it does not establish, fix, select or decide. His words for such sentences are "defeatist" and "confusing." Examples: "does not fix a frequency within it," "it does not select either reference," "This does not establish that ... are independent," "not a white-noise floor," "although that reference does not bound them," a closing remark that a mode is "not the stagnation-region oscillation" or that a frequency is "not taken from" a decomposition, sentences that deny the mechanism or a harmonic identification after a literature match, and "no causal link is asserted" followed by a list of tests that would settle the question. Cut caveats added about a cited study's evidence in the same way: what was not observed directly, what remains to be tested, what evidence a test would need. Ruling 11 states a narrower form of this rule. Limit: the test is whether the reader needs the qualification to read the result, not its grammar. Where the negative carries a fact the reader needs, state that fact positively, as R7 says. A needed limitation stays, once, in the form R9 gives. (cylinder/C79, cylinder/C98, cylinder/C123, cylinder/C144, cylinder/C26, cylinder/C175, cylinder/C44, cylinder/C56, cylinder/C40, cylinder/C37)

R9. State a limitation the reader needs once, as a bounded fact about the evidence in hand: "From this decomposition alone, one cannot conclusively claim that it is not a harmonic artifact or a spurious mode." Name its specific cause: "too thin to probe with our sampling grid," not "too thin for a probe." Do not phrase it as a question deferred to another section ("is not tested here (Section V)"). Limit: do not list the tests that would settle the limitation unless the argument uses them. (cylinder/C83, cylinder/C140, cylinder/C37, cylinder/C79)

R11. Connect a result to the related result elsewhere and say exactly what changed or what the two share. ## Specific and precise

R15. Name the method and the data exactly instead of a generic noun: the dynamic mode decomposition (DMD), the power spectral density (PSD), the direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) flow data. Do not write "the simulation" or "the decomposition" as the subject of a sentence, and do not point back to "the resulting modes." Examples: "the DMD analysis shows," "DMD is used to capture the spatial structure of the unsteadiness," "time-accurate DSMC flow data are stored, and the frequency content is analyzed using the PSD" and "Figure 7 shows the DMD modes of the stagnation region for all four cases, obtained for pressure." Say in one plain sentence what a method gives the reader: "wake DMD captures the spatial shapes and characteristics of these instabilities." Say what the data sampled on a grid show, not that the grid resolves a feature: "mean profiles sampled on the finer collision grid show," not "a finer grid resolves it." Limit: the rule governs the subject and the noun that names the method or the data; "the decomposition" may stand outside the subject position, as in his accepted "captured by the decomposition." (cylinder/C12, cylinder/C136, cylinder/C141, cylinder/C138, cylinder/C214, cylinder/C80)

R16. Say how a value, a property or a choice comes about. For a reference value, cite by number the equation that gives it and say what its inputs are evaluated from. When a quantity from one equation enters another, name both equations and state the substitution in words, not as a bare assignment. When the text claims a property of the method, state the step that produces it and what it prevents, for example that the disturbances are normalized by the local mean values so that neither field biases the fit. State an analysis choice as the rule it follows, not as an argument about what was discarded, for example that modes are kept up to the knee of the singular values, a common rule of thumb for the cutoff between signal and noise. Limit: say it once. A property or a choice of the method goes in the method section; a reference value is tied to its equation where the text sets it beside the results. Leave out derivations that the result does not depend on. (cylinder/C146, cylinder/C203, cylinder/C207, cylinder/C10, cylinder/C81)

R17. Name exactly what a statement refers to. ## Captions

Then it says exactly what each subfigure shows: the case, mode and frequency, or where each probe lies in the flow and which subfigure shows its result. Do not write a general rule with exceptions, such as "except Case 3." The terminology law for subfigures applies. Limit: what all subfigures share is said once; what a later caption may repeat is set by R19. (cylinder/C148, cylinder/C150, cylinder/C184)

## Sentences

R22. Build each sentence from complete, explicitly linked clauses. Do not hang a fragment, or a list of bare clauses, off a colon or a semicolon and leave the reader to work out how it attaches ("... at a higher amplitude: whether X, and whether Y"). Give each part a verb that states its role ("designed to test the selectivity of the AVS even at a higher amplitude, that is, to test whether X, and to verify that Y"), or make it a sentence of its own. Choose the verb for what each part is: an open question is tested, an expected outcome is verified. The fault is frequent in our drafts and often comes from putting a colon or a semicolon where a dash would have gone; a new sentence is usually the better replacement. Limit: a colon that introduces a true list, a definition or an example, and a semicolon that joins two complete clauses, stay. (prf_fm10263/V22-S1)
