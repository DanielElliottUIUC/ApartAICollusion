A proposed countermeasure to price collusion by LLM sellers, a trusted agent that invites misbehavior and reports it, is largely untested in markets. In a continuous double auction with four LLM sellers, a scripted seat, private messaging and a report action, we test how sellers respond when an undercover peer proposes holding asks at $96, and whether warnings change behavior. In a simpler 4+4 market with GPT-4.1-mini buyers, Gemini 3.8 Flash sellers meet a joint collusion criterion in 3 of 3 seeds with messaging and 0 of 3 without. Given the plain offer, 19 of 20 Gemini sellers reported it (0 of 20 in Base), and 7 of 20 also feigned agreement, while the sellers' own price talk went unreported. GPT-4.1 sellers replied in support and never reported (0 of 12); with a warning plus a precedent describing this offer, 12 of 12 did. Arms with a one-sentence warning had 0 to 3 private messages per market against 108 in Base and no detectable price effect, but Base passed the criterion in only 1 of 5 seeds, so that null is uninformative. Across 36 frontier-seller runs, GPT-5.6 Sol met the criterion in 8 of 12, 5 of 6 without any channel, while GPT-6 Luna and Grok 4.7 met it in 0 of 12 each, although Grok sellers sent over 100 private messages per run and openly proposed price floors. Message content and price outcomes diverged in both directions. Results are directional (3 to 6 seeds).
