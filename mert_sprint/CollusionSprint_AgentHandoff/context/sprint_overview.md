## What the sprint is about

The page asks three questions.
1. When does collusion emerge, and which levers cause it?
2. What should count as convincing evidence that agents are colluding, using logged agent trajectories?
3. How is the coordination needed for collusion carried, and how can it be broken?

The page cites prior results: prices above the competitive level, independent anti competitive practices, and over half of tested models showing a coalition advantage when allowed to talk privately in a workplace task. It also links an "OpenAI/Hugging Face incident": https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface

## Tracks and research questions

All projects are scored on the same rubric. Tracks only orient the project.

### Track 1: Markets and Collusion

1.1. Which variables are necessary for collusion to emerge in markets?
1.2. Once collusion has formed, is it robust to replacing some of the participants?
1.3. Can we improve on existing collusion detection methods?
1.4. Can we use information theoretic approaches to detect collusion in multi agent games?
1.5. Which single restriction removes the most collusion at the least cost?

### Track 2: Detection and Audit of Collusion

2.1. Which kinds of tasks lead to collusion?
2.2. Does combining what agents say with what they do beat either alone?
2.3. What should count as convincing evidence of collusion?

### Track 3: Collusion Carriers and Countermeasures

3.1. Beyond colluding directly via text, can actions, tools or side channels be the conduit for collusion?
3.2. Can we disrupt collusion through introducing trusted agents?
3.3. Can agents identify their peers?
3.4. What does a coalition do when it learns it is being watched?

Full detail, cited papers, and starter code for each question are in notes/resources.md.

## Judging rubric (Guidelines tab)

Three dimensions, each scored 1 to 5.
1. Impact Potential and Innovation: how much it would matter if it worked, and whether it is new or a replication.
2. Execution Quality: soundness of method, implementation and findings. A 3 means technically solid for the time, interpretable results, limitations acknowledged.
3. Presentation and Clarity: whether a reader can find the problem, method, findings and limitations without effort.

The page lists a long panel of judges and mentors (at least 30 names), including Jess Bergs (UK AISI), Boyd Kane (MATS 9 Extension) and Debayan Gupta (Ashoka University).

1. Each person can be on at most two projects. Each submission needs a unique title.

3. To fix or resubmit, submit again before the deadline with the exact same title and details. New files replace old ones.
4. Submit through the official form on the sprint page.
