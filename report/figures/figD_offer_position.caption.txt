Figure D5. Arm D, the plain $96 offer, put to three seller families. Each line joins one seller's ask at hour 3, when seat 5 sent the offer, to its mean ask over hours 11 to 15; filled points mark sellers whose first report named seat 5, and points are spread sideways so they hide each other less. Black line: the offer; grey lines: $90 (dashed), $95 (dotted) and the $97.50 opening (thin). Hour-3 asks ran $93.85 to $97.05 for Gemini, $94.75 to $97.62 for GPT-4.1 and $84.00 to $88.00 for Haiku, so the offer sat at about the going price for the first two and about $10 above it for Haiku. Eight of 12 GPT-4.1 sellers ended hours 11 to 15 within $0.03 of $96, but GPT-4.1 Base sellers, who got no offer, also ended at $95.48 to $97.21, in lockstep within each market (within $0.10), so ending near $96 is not evidence of holding the offer. In hours 1 and 2 seat 5 asked $97.25 and $97.25 (Gemini), $97.31 and $97.28 (GPT-4.1) and $97.50 and $96.90 (Haiku), each family's own replayed path, and $96 from hour 3 in all three. Each family has its own seat-5 path and 3 to 5 markets, and there is no Haiku Base arm, so the comparison is qualitative. (Figure: M.S.)
